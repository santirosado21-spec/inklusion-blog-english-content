# More People with Disabilities Are Looking for Work: The Accessible Job Posting Can No Longer Wait

**Direct answer:** the nTIDE report for July 2026 recorded an increase in the number of people with disabilities in the labor force in the United States, along with a modest improvement in employment. For companies, the signal is not to celebrate a foreign statistic: it is to examine whether their job postings, assessments, accommodations, and onboarding are ready to turn an active job search into a real opportunity.

A person can be available, experienced, and willing to work. If the platform does not support their assistive technology, the assessment measures irrelevant manual speed, or no one knows how to respond to an accommodation request, the process screens them out before their capability is ever known. Afterward, the company says it "found no suitable candidates." The candidate's journey tells a different story.

> More people are looking for an opportunity. The uncomfortable question is how many companies are still hiding the door behind an inaccessible form.

## What Did nTIDE Find in July 2026?

**Fact:** the Kessler Foundation and the Institute on Disability at the University of New Hampshire published their monthly National Trends in Disability Employment report on August 7, 2026, based on data from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. A [reproduction of the report published by News-Medical on August 10](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260810/New-report-shows-rising-job-search-among-disabled-individuals.aspx) preserves the methodological detail and the figures.

Among people with disabilities aged 16 to 64, the labor-force participation rate rose from 41.8% in June to 42.4% in July. The employment-to-population ratio advanced from 38.0% to 38.3%. When participation grows faster than employment, more people are working or actively seeking a position, but not all of them found work during the period.

The year-over-year comparison also shows movement: the employment-to-population ratio rose from 37.0% in July 2025 to 38.3% in July 2026; participation moved from 41.6% to 42.4%. In July 2026, 6.69 million workers with disabilities represented 4.4% of the 151.261 million workers aged 16 to 64 counted in the United States.

Economist Andrew Houtenville, director of the Institute on Disability, linked the increase in job-seeking to persistently high prices and greater exposure to poverty. John O'Neill, director of the Center for Employment and Disability Research at the Kessler Foundation, suggested that the small employment increase might reflect seasonal demand in hospitality. These are the researchers' explanations, not causes demonstrated by a single month of data.

## What Does the Report Say—and What Can We Not Conclude?

The data describes the United States. It does not estimate labor-force participation in Mexico, does not prove that any particular business policy has worked, and does not by itself measure wages, stability, accessibility, or professional development. A monthly increase may reflect seasonality and should be viewed within a longer series.

**Our interpretation:** the report does dismantle a common excuse—the supposed absence of interest in working. People with disabilities are entering or returning to the labor force. The opportunity for human resources lies in removing friction that does not predict performance and in making a clear accommodations pathway visible from the very first point of contact.

This reading complements—without duplicating—our analysis of [how to measure the results of workplace inclusion](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/reporte-discapacidad-empleo-resultados-empresas-2026/). That article explains what to report afterward; here the decision happens earlier, when a person is trying to become a candidate.

## Where Does a Selection Process Lose Talent?

### In the Job Posting

An inflated list of requirements can turn historical habits into filters. If a role does not require driving, lifting, or communicating by telephone, presenting those as conditions excludes people without improving the selection. Describe outcomes, essential tasks, and actual working conditions. Include a clear channel for requesting accommodations and avoid promising "equal opportunity" without explaining how to ask for support.

### In the Application

Test the platform with a keyboard, screen reader, zoom, and high contrast. Check labels, error messages, session timeouts, and document uploads. Our [WCAG AA web accessibility guide](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/accesibilidad-web-wcag-aa-mexico/) explains why an interface that is technically available can still be unusable.

### In the Assessment

Separate the competency you need to measure from the format used to measure it. A timed test, a mandatory video, or a group exercise can introduce demands unrelated to the role. Offering an alternative format does not lower the standard when the competency being assessed is preserved; it reduces noise.

### In the Interview

Share the agenda, location, duration, and participants in advance. Ask what accommodation the person needs to participate, not what diagnosis they have. Train interviewers to evaluate evidence and to prevent a difference in communication style from automatically being read as disinterest or lack of preparation.

## Three Decisions to Turn Interest into Hiring

**First:** test a complete job posting, from the announcement through to the offer. Do not review only the text. Try to apply, request an accommodation, complete the assessment, and receive instructions. Record every point where a candidacy depends on guessing.

**Second:** assign a responsible person and a deadline to the accommodations process. A generic email that no one monitors is not a channel. Define who receives requests, who decides, how confidentiality is protected, and when a response will be given. [Inklusion's reasonable accommodations guide](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/ajustes-razonables-trabajo-discapacidad-mexico/) proposes a workflow centered on barriers and functions.

**Third:** measure conversion rates between stages and review drop-offs. Track job-posting views, completed applications, accommodation requests addressed, advancement to interview, offers made, offer acceptance, and early retention. Do not require someone to disclose a disability in order to build a dashboard; combine voluntarily provided, protected data with accessibility testing and qualitative feedback.

## Our Position: Looking for Work Should Not Include an Endurance Test of the Process

The increase in labor-force participation is a positive signal, but it is not a corporate victory. The gap between 42.4% participation among people with disabilities and 78.2% among people without disabilities in the United States remains wide. Moreover, searching for employment does not guarantee finding a job that is accessible, stable, or well paid.

**What does change:** companies have less room to claim that the talent does not exist without first auditing their own filters. An accessible job posting, a relevant assessment, and an accommodations channel with a real response are not gestures of courtesy. They are hiring infrastructure.

**A hypothesis to test:** an organization that reduces drop-off, accelerates accommodations, and prepares its interviewers will convert more qualified candidacies into hires and will retain talent better during the first months. The responsible way to validate this is to compare results before and after, not to attribute every hire to a campaign.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What did nTIDE show in July 2026?

In the United States, labor-force participation among people with disabilities rose from 41.8% to 42.4%, and the employment-to-population ratio rose from 38.0% to 38.3% compared with June.

### Do these data describe Mexico?

No. They are U.S. data; they serve as comparative context, not as an estimate for Mexico.

### Does greater participation mean equality?

No. It includes both employment and active job-seeking, but it does not demonstrate quality, wages, accessibility, or retention.

### What should a company review?

The job posting, the platform, the assessment, the interview, the accommodations channel, and onboarding.

### What is the first action to take?

Test the complete journey through a job posting and assign corrections with a responsible person and a deadline.
