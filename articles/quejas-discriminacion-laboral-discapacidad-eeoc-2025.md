# Disability Discrimination at Work: Charges Rose in the U.S. — and the Data Demands a Look at Process

**Direct answer:** the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) recorded 36,256 disability charges in fiscal year 2025, up 7.7% from the 33,668 filed in 2024. This is not a measurement of Mexico, nor does it prove that every allegation involved discrimination. It does signal that employment decisions and accommodations require verifiable processes.

The figure matters less as a ranking and more as a management question: when a person encounters a barrier to applying for a job, performing work, or advancing, does the organization know how to receive that person, make a decision, and document a response — without turning the process itself into another barrier?

> The risk does not begin when a complaint arrives. It begins when no one can explain who receives a request, how it is evaluated, and when a response is due.

## What Do the EEOC Data Actually Show?

**Fact:** in March 2026 the EEOC published [Table E6a on charges and resolutions under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)](https://www.eeoc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/Table_E6a._ADA_%28Disability%29_Charge_Receipts_and_Resolutions_by_Type_of_Resolution_FY_1992_-_FY_2025.xlsx). The file records 36,256 charges received in FY2025. In FY2024 the figure was 33,668. The difference is 2,588 charges, equivalent to an increase of 7.7%.

The same table reports 35,317 resolutions in 2025, 7,018 "merit resolutions," and $192.05 million in monetary benefits, not including benefits obtained through litigation. These rows must not be added together or interpreted as if they all described the same cases from the same year: the EEOC clarifies that resolutions may include matters pending from prior periods.

There are also scope limitations. The data aggregate charges filed under the cited statute, basis, or issue, even when filed concurrently with other bases. They also exclude charges filed with state or local fair employment practice agencies. A spreadsheet can look very tidy; administrative reality rarely fits in a single column.

## Does the Increase Mean There Was 7.7% More Discrimination?

We cannot assert that. A charge is an allegation received, not a verdict or an automatic finding that a violation occurred. The change may also be influenced by greater awareness of rights, access to the system, labor conditions, institutional capacity to receive cases, or a combination of factors that this table does not disaggregate.

**Editorial interpretation:** the data do rule out the comfortable notion that disability discrimination is a residual problem. Tens of thousands of people initiated a formal process in a single year within the United States. For a company, waiting for certainty about the macro-level cause before reviewing its own friction points would be confusing statistical prudence with passivity.

Responsible reading holds two ideas at once: do not treat charges as automatic proof of guilt, and do not minimize the signal because the data have limitations. Measuring carefully does not mean looking away until a perfect experiment is available.

## What Can a Mexican Company Learn Without Importing a Foreign Law?

The ADA and the EEOC belong to the U.S. legal framework. This article does not transfer their rules to Mexico or offer a Mexican legal interpretation. The useful lesson is operational: conflicts tend to surface at specific moments in the employment cycle — application, interview, accommodation request, performance review, promotion, or termination — and each moment can be better designed.

The EEOC's guidance for [job applicants and the ADA](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/job-applicants-and-ada) explains, within its jurisdiction, that accommodations may be necessary to participate in a selection process and to perform the essential functions of a position. The [International Labour Organization](https://www.ilo.org/publications/promoting-diversity-and-inclusion-through-workplace-adjustments-practical) addresses reasonable adjustments more broadly across the entire employment cycle and presents them as a way to provide equal opportunity.

For an organization in Mexico, these materials serve as design references, not as a substitute for local legal advice. They make it possible to ask whether a job posting describes real functions, whether the accommodations channel is visible, whether an interview can be adapted, and whether a manager knows how to distinguish a barrier from a performance issue.

## Four Points Where a Policy Can Fail

### 1. Recruitment That Declares Inclusion but Offers No Support Pathway

A phrase like "we are an inclusive company" does not help a person who needs an interpreter, additional time, an accessible format, or an alternative to the application system. The job posting should provide a contact and explain what information is needed to arrange the accommodation, without requiring the disclosure of unnecessary clinical details.

### 2. Essential Functions Mixed with Habits

"Excellent presentation," "full availability," or "ability to work under pressure" often appear without definition. If a condition does not change the expected outcome of the role, it may describe a historical preference rather than an essential function. Our [guide to writing an inclusive job description](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/descripcion-puesto-inclusiva-discapacidad-guia/) proposes separating outcomes, activities, and verifiable conditions.

### 3. Accommodations Managed by Improvisation

When every request depends on a manager's sensitivity, two people with similar barriers may receive opposite responses. What is needed is a process with intake, analysis, alternatives, a decision, a timeline, confidentiality, and follow-up. [Inklusion's guide to reasonable adjustments](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/ajustes-razonables-trabajo-discapacidad-mexico/) helps center the conversation on function, barrier, and effectiveness.

### 4. Evidence That Only Appears After a Conflict

Documenting does not mean building a file against the person. It means preserving agreements, responsible parties, dates, alternatives, and outcomes so the organization can tell whether a barrier was addressed. Without evidence, a company cannot distinguish an isolated case from a recurring delay in technology, procurement, or leadership.

## What Should a Talent Committee Review This Quarter?

A useful dashboard does not need to expose diagnoses. It can track how many requests arrive at each stage, how long the first response takes, how many are resolved within the agreed timeframe, which barriers recur, and what percentage of critical processes was tested with people with disabilities.

It is worth segmenting by process and area when privacy allows. An average of five days may conceal the fact that recruitment responds in one day while technology takes twenty. It is also important to review application drop-offs, date changes, internal complaints, and closure reasons, without assuming that every drop-off is related to disability.

**Hypothesis to test:** a visible accommodations pathway, well-defined job functions, and measured response times will reduce friction, rework, and conflict escalation. The organization must test this with its own data and accessible feedback; the EEOC figure does not predict the outcome for a Mexican organization.

## The Position: Compliance and Experience Must Not Live Apart

Treating discrimination solely as a legal matter means the review starts too late. Treating it solely as a culture issue leaves important decisions without an owner, a deadline, or evidence. Workplace inclusion needs both disciplines: clear principles and processes that work when a person actually uses them.

The rise in charges in the United States does not allow us to diagnose Mexico. It does offer a timely warning: a company does not control how many barriers exist simply by having published a policy. It controls what it designs, how it responds, and what it learns when the system fails.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many disability charges did the EEOC receive in FY2025?
36,256, compared with 33,668 in FY2024: an increase of 7.7%.

### Does a charge prove discrimination?
No. It is an allegation received; it is not equivalent to a verdict or an automatic finding of a violation.

### Do the data represent Mexico?
No. They correspond to the United States and the ADA framework. They are an international signal, not a Mexican statistic.

### Do more charges necessarily mean more incidents?
Not necessarily. The table does not identify the cause of the change, and multiple factors may be involved.

### Which process should be reviewed first?
The accommodations channel: visibility, intake, timelines, confidentiality, alternatives, decision, and follow-up.
