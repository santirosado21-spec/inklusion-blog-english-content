# WCAG-EM 2 Expands Accessibility Evaluation: the Product No Longer Ends at the Web Page

**Direct answer:** the World Wide Web Consortium published WCAG-EM 2 on July 23, 2026, to evaluate the conformance of digital products, including websites, mobile applications, and kiosks. The change matters because a company can no longer review only individual pages when the actual service spans multiple channels. The methodology structures scope, sample, evaluation, and reporting; it does not create new WCAG requirements.

A person may discover a service on the web, register through an application, validate their identity with a document, and complete the process at a kiosk. If the audit looks only at the home page, it will have evaluated one visible piece while leaving intact the barrier that determines whether the transaction is completed.

> The useful unit of evaluation is not the easiest screen to show. It is the product and the processes a person needs to complete.

## What did W3C publish and what is its scope?

The W3C Accessibility Guidelines Working Group published the [WCAG Evaluation Methodology (WCAG-EM) 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG-EM/) as a Group Note on July 23, 2026. The document proposes a common process for determining how well a digital product conforms to WCAG 2.

The [official W3C introduction](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/conformance/wcag-em/) identifies the main difference from WCAG-EM 1: the first version was focused on websites and web pages; WCAG-EM 2 can also be applied to applications and other digital products. W3C expressly mentions sites, mobile apps, and kiosks.

**Fact:** WCAG-EM 2 expanded the applicability of the methodology. **Our interpretation:** the update corrects an artificial separation that many organizations still maintain between "the web" and "the product." For a user, the channel changes; the need to complete the task does not.

## What does not change with WCAG-EM 2?

WCAG-EM 2 does not replace WCAG, does not modify its success criteria, and does not add requirements. It explains how to organize an evaluation: define what will be reviewed, explore the product, select a representative sample, evaluate it, and report results.

It also does not turn an automated scan into a complete audit. W3C notes that the methodology requires knowledge of WCAG, accessible design, assistive technologies, and the ways in which people with different disabilities use digital products. Tools help, but most checks cannot be fully automated.

And there is a nuance that is often lost amid badges and percentages: evaluating a sample does not prove that every screen of the complete product conforms. W3C warns that a WCAG conformance claim for the entire scope cannot be based solely on a subset, because errors may exist outside the sample.

## How does it organize a digital accessibility evaluation?

### 1. Define the scope and objective

The team agrees on which product falls within the evaluation, what conformance level it is targeting, and what baseline of assistive technologies it will consider. "The app" is not sufficient as a scope if sign-up opens an external page or if the receipt arrives as a PDF.

### 2. Explore the actual product

The methodology calls for identifying common views, essential functions, variety of content, technologies used, and other relevant samples. In a mobile banking app, checking a balance may be frequent; recovering access may be less frequent, but decisive.

### 3. Select a representative sample

When reviewing every view is not feasible, WCAG-EM 2 combines a structured sample with a random one and includes complete processes. The selection ceases to be a collection of convenient screens and becomes a documented decision.

### 4. Evaluate views and processes

The applicable WCAG criteria, conforming alternative versions, accessibility support, and non-interference are reviewed. Complete processes receive their own attention: it is not enough for each isolated step to appear correct if the flow breaks when context changes.

### 5. Report what was done and what was found

The report documents scope, sample, results, and limitations. The methodology optionally contemplates an evaluation statement, an aggregate score, and machine-readable reports. A figure can facilitate tracking; it must never hide a critical barrier behind a friendly average.

## What changes for product, procurement, and compliance?

For product teams, the change is practical: the component inventory must become a journey inventory. Sign-in, search, payment, download, signing, support, and account recovery typically cross technologies and vendors.

For procurement, a vendor's accessibility statement remains entry-level evidence, not the end of the review. The organization needs to agree on scope, environment, version, essential functions, evaluation method, and defect handling. Our [guide to buying accessible software](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/comprar-software-accesible-checklist-proveedores/) explains how to bring those criteria into the selection process and the contract.

For compliance and audit purposes, WCAG-EM 2 offers a common language for repeating evaluations and comparing results. However, it is important to emphasize the legal boundary: it is a technical methodology from W3C, not Mexican law. Each organization must review the obligations that apply to its sector, contracts, and context.

## Four decisions worth making now

- **Redraw the scope:** document which sites, apps, kiosks, documents, and third-party services make up each critical journey.
- **Define essential functions:** prioritize tasks whose failure prevents a person from registering, purchasing, working, studying, requesting help, or completing a transaction.
- **Separate finding from declaration:** state precisely what was evaluated, what was left out, and what can be asserted with the available evidence.
- **Combine methods:** integrate technical review, manual testing, and participation by people with disabilities; no single layer answers all questions on its own.

If the organization already has a [web accessibility audit](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/auditoria-accesibilidad-web-wcag-checklist-mexico/), the next step is not to discard it. It is to review whether the scope includes the product people actually use.

## Our position: evaluating by org chart produces incomplete diagnoses

Many companies divide web, applications, documents, and customer service among different owners. That structure may serve to manage work, but it does not represent a person's experience. The barrier appears precisely at the seam: a link that opens another domain, an external CAPTCHA, an unstructured PDF, or a kiosk that does not preserve the accessible alternative.

**Hypothesis:** organizations that evaluate complete processes will find more transition barriers and will be better able to prioritize those that prevent a task from being completed. This must be verified by comparing findings, abandonment rates, recurrence, and correction times before and after the methodological change.

WCAG-EM 2 does not make a product accessible by being published. It does make less defensible an audit that cherry-picks the easy screens, ignores connected channels, and presents the sample as if it were the whole.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is WCAG-EM 2?

A W3C methodology for evaluating, through a common process, how well a digital product conforms to WCAG 2.

### What products can it evaluate?

Websites, mobile applications, kiosks, and other complete digital products, with a defined scope.

### Does it add new requirements to WCAG?

No. It organizes the evaluation, but does not add or modify the requirements of WCAG 2.

### Does a sample allow a conformance claim for the entire product?

Not necessarily. A sample can support an evaluation result, but does not demonstrate that every view outside it conforms.

### Is it a legal obligation for Mexican companies?

Not on its own. WCAG-EM 2 is a technical methodology; the legal analysis depends on each organization's context.
