# Inclusive job description: how to open a vacancy without lowering the standard

**Direct answer:** an inclusive job description defines outcomes and essential functions, distinguishes mandatory requirements from skills that can be learned, describes real working conditions without assuming a single way of working, and offers an accessible channel for requesting adjustments. It uses plain language, accessible formats, and consistent evaluation criteria. Adding a diversity statement without fixing the filters does not make a vacancy inclusive.

Many vacancies are written by copying the previous version. That is how requirements no one remembers deciding survive: five years of experience for a task that can be learned in one, "excellent presentation," permanent availability, or the ability to carry heavy loads even though the role is spent almost entirely in front of a computer.

> The standard must protect the outcome of the role, not the habit of how the last person performed it.

## What changes in an inclusive job description?

The precision changes. Instead of describing the ideal candidate as a collection of traits, it explains what the person must achieve, which activities are fundamental, who they work with, what tools they use, and under what real conditions they operate.

The [International Labour Organization](https://www.ilo.org/publications/promoting-diversity-and-inclusion-through-workplace-adjustments-practical) notes that adjustments can include modifications to the job, machinery, work content, time and organization, and that they should be considered throughout the entire employment cycle. The EEOC guidance on [job applicants and the ADA](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/job-applicants-and-ada), applicable in the United States, distinguishes essential functions and recognizes adjustments for competing for employment.

These references help in designing processes, but they do not replace a legal review in Mexico. The practical decision is useful in any case: describe the outcome before turning a specific way of achieving it into a requirement.

## How do you separate an essential function from a preference?

Start with verbs and outcomes. "Deliver a weekly inventory report with validated data" is more useful than "be extremely organized." "Handle customer conversations through the defined channels and record agreements" says more than "have excellent communication."

Then ask which part is indispensable. If the role requires moving product frequently, document the weight, frequency, environment, and reason. If the outcome can be achieved with equipment, redistribution, or a different tool, do not turn a historical technique into the only option before evaluating alternatives.

A brief review can use four questions:

- What outcome would cease to exist if we removed this activity?
- How often does it occur and how much time does it take?
- Is there another effective way to achieve the same outcome?
- Does the requirement belong to day one, or can it be learned within a reasonable timeframe?

The goal is not to erase demands. It is to be able to justify why each demand exists.

## What requirements drive away talent without improving selection?

### Years of experience used as a substitute for competence

Years can be a useful guide when accumulated exposure is relevant, but they do not guarantee mastery. Combine them with observable evidence: solving cases, operating a tool, coordinating a process, or making decisions under defined criteria. If you accept equivalencies between experience, education, and portfolio, write it down.

### Total availability without a real schedule

State the working hours, shifts, travel, on-site requirements, and foreseeable variations. "Schedule availability" forces every person to imagine the most restrictive scenario and makes it harder to evaluate adjustments or caregiving responsibilities.

### Ambiguous traits

"Excellent presentation," "high energy," "tolerance for pressure," or "winning personality" do not explain performance and open the door to bias. Replace them with behaviors and conditions: a dress code if necessary, workload volume, response times, interaction with the public, and quality criteria.

### Technologies and credentials that do not correspond to the work

If a tool can be learned during onboarding, mark it as desirable or explain the level required. If a specific credential is required by regulation, state it; if it only functions as a shortcut to reduce applications, review what competency you are actually trying to measure.

## How do you talk about disability without paternalism?

Use "people with disabilities" unless a community or individual prefers another term. Avoid expressions such as "different abilities," "despite their disability," or "inspiration." A vacancy is looking for talent for a job, not protagonists for a campaign.

Do not promise "zero barriers" or claim that all facilities, tools, and stages are accessible if they have not been verified. It is better to be specific: step-free entrance, elevator, accessible restroom, screen-reader compatibility tested, remote interview available, or a channel for arranging adjustments.

The equal opportunity statement can reinforce the message, but it must be accompanied by actions. For example:

> We welcome applications from people with disabilities. If you need an adjustment to participate in any stage, contact us at [channel]. We will use the information solely to arrange the support and will respond within [timeframe].

Adapt the text with internal and legal guidance. What matters is that the channel exists, is monitored, and does not lead to an inbox no one checks.

## Is the vacancy and the application process also accessible?

Publish the listing as accessible HTML whenever possible. If you add a PDF, make sure it has headings, reading order, real text, tags, and sufficient contrast; our [accessible PDF guide](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/como-crear-pdf-accesible-checklist-empresas/) details the process.

The application path must be completable with a keyboard, screen reader, zoom, and mobile device. Fields need labels, errors must explain what happened, and time limits must be manageable. The [accessible forms guide](https://inklusion-blog.vercel.app/blog/formularios-accesibles-checklist-wcag/) covers those points without assuming that an automated test is sufficient.

Offer an equivalent alternative channel when the platform fails and explain what will happen after submitting. Asking a person to report the inaccessibility of an inaccessible portal from within that same portal has an almost poetic efficiency, but it does not resolve the application.

## Before and after: from ambiguous filter to useful criterion

**Before:** "We are looking for a dynamic executive with excellent presentation, tolerance for pressure, full availability, own vehicle, and five years of experience. Must be proficient in Excel and perform other functions."

**After:** "The person will manage a portfolio of up to 40 accounts, record agreements in the CRM, and prepare a weekly report. Standard hours are Monday to Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.; there are two monthly visits within the Metropolitan Area and the company covers transportation. Demonstrable experience in commercial follow-up and spreadsheet use for filters and pivot tables is required; the specific tool can be learned during the first month."

The second version maintains expectations. It also makes it possible to evaluate evidence, anticipate conditions, and discuss alternatives. The change does not "make the role easier": it reduces noise in the selection process.

## A review process before publishing

**First, validate with the hiring manager.** Ask for three expected outcomes within 90 days and the essential activities. Do not accept a list of adjectives as a substitute.

**Then, review with talent and inclusion teams.** Classify requirements as mandatory, desirable, or learnable. Identify ambiguous language, unexplained conditions, and possible adjustments.

**Test the complete path.** Open the vacancy, navigate it, apply, trigger errors, and request support. Include people with disabilities in the evaluation whenever possible and compensate their participation.

**Define consistent evaluation.** Use the same competencies and baseline questions for comparable applications. A structured process does not eliminate all bias, but it reduces the freedom to change the standard depending on who is sitting across from the interviewer.

## How do you know whether the new vacancy is working?

Measure applications that meet the criteria, progression by stage, drop-offs, form errors, adjustment requests, response time, and offer acceptance. Review qualitative feedback to find confusing requirements or conditions discovered too late.

If you collect disability self-identification, it must be voluntary, confidential, separate from individual decisions, and tied to a clear purpose. A dashboard with sensitive data but no action is not inclusion; it is inventory.

**Hypothesis to test:** by separating essential functions from preferences and making the adjustments channel visible, qualified applications from people who previously self-excluded will increase. Test it by comparing similar periods and roles, without attributing every variation to a single text change.

## Quick checklist for the next posting

- The title describes the role without internal codes.
- The opening paragraphs explain the purpose and outcomes.
- Essential functions are separated from occasional tasks.
- Each mandatory requirement has a reason tied to the work.
- Learnable requirements appear as desirable or with a timeframe.
- Schedule, location, travel, and physical conditions are concrete.
- The language avoids vague traits, paternalism, and unverified promises.
- An accessible adjustments channel exists with a responsible party and a response timeframe.
- The listing and the application form work with a keyboard and assistive technologies.
- The interview criteria correspond to what was published.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an inclusive job description?

A precise description of outcomes, functions, requirements, and conditions that removes filters unrelated to the work and offers adjustments.

### Does it mean lowering requirements?

No. It means keeping the necessary ones and removing inherited preferences that do not improve the outcome.

### Is it a good idea to write "vacancy for people with disabilities"?

Only with a specific reason and strategy. In general, communicate openness, accessibility, and adjustments without treating people as a homogeneous group.

### What should the adjustments channel say?

How to make contact, which stages it covers, what minimum information is needed, how confidentiality is protected, and when a response will be provided.

### How is it measured?

Through qualified applications, drop-offs, supports requested, response time, progression by stage, and feedback.
